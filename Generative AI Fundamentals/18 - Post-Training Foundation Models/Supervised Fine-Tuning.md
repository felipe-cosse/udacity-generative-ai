Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is an important post-training method for foundation models. It aims to optimize a pre-trained model for conversational tasks and instruction following, rather than just simple text completion.

Pre-trained models, often called base models, are initially trained on vast, web-scraped data. This process makes them adept at predicting the next token in a sequence but leaves them struggling to produce helpful, natural dialogues or follow specific instructions accurately. SFT corrects these behavioral issues by teaching the model what "good responses" look like. It can also be used to teach a model domain-specific terminology, such as specialized healthcare concepts, that may have been lacking in its general pre-training data.

The SFT process involves taking a pre-trained model and further training it on a high-quality, curated dataset of instructions. This dataset, known as "demonstration data," consists of prompt-response pairs. For example, a prompt might be "summarize this document," and the ideal response would be a curated, high-quality summary.

This step is sometimes called "behavior cloning" because the model learns to mimic or clone the desired behaviors demonstrated in the examples. The demonstration data should encompass the full range of tasks the model is expected to handle, such as question answering, summarization, or translation.

SFT provides several key benefits:

Improved Instruction Following: It significantly enhances a model's ability to adhere to specific output styles and formats, like JSON or YAML.
Enhanced Domain-Specific Capabilities: Models can be fine-tuned to perform better on niche tasks, such as coding or medical question-answering, by exposing them to relevant data.
Bias Mitigation: Using carefully curated data during SFT can help counteract biases that were present in the base model's original, more general training data.
Model Distillation: SFT is often used to train smaller models to imitate the behavior of larger, more powerful models. This makes the smaller models more efficient and cost-effective for specific tasks.
While SFT greatly improves a model's ability to produce instruction-aligned responses, it relies on having explicit demonstrations. Defining ideal responses for every possible scenario is often impractical or even impossible. This limitation motivates the use of a complementary approach called preference tuning, which further aligns model outputs by incorporating nuanced human preferences and subjective evaluations.

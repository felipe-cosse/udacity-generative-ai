Evaluating Agent Personas
You've crafted your perfect persona prompt. Now, how do you know if the LLM is actually doing a good job of following the role and other specifications you’ve set for it?

Evaluation is key!
It's similar to a director assessing an actor's performance during rehearsals. You need to test the AI's responses to see if they are consistent with your expectations. For instance, is the tone/format correct, or is the answer even accurate?

Ground Truth Evaluations
The most straightforward, effective, and reliable way is to create a ground truth set for scoring future outputs.

Start by defining the task and identifying the criteria for success. What does "good" output look like? Does it require factual accuracy, specific formatting, or a particular tone?
Based on this, develop a set of test inputs and the ideal outputs – your ground truth.
When your AI Agent generates a response, compare it to the ground truth.
For structured data like JSON, use checks to ensure the agent adheres to the expected format and values. Start with traditional Machine Learning evaluation metrics such as completeness, accuracy, precision, and recall.
For textual responses or even images, which are more freeform, you may employ another LLM as a judge (LLM-as-a-judge), feeding it both the agent's output and the ground truth and asking it to score the agent based on your provided criteria. Note, in this case, you will need to evaluate the evaluator!
In all these cases, it’s important to continue to view the raw inputs and outputs of your models, sometimes called traces. This will give you a better understanding of what is actually going on and help you catch errors your evaluation pipeline may miss.
Other Evaluation Types
Other evaluation methods not always based on a ground truth dataset are also important:

Consistency & Persona Adherence: Is the agent staying within its defined role? This is essential, for instance, in the case of a Customer Support agent. If the agent is supposed to be a hospital assistant, does it stay within that role, even if asked about non-medical topics? A mock conversation can be passed to the Agent and the output can be evaluated by another LLM.
Robustness Testing: Assess resilience against adversarial attacks. This is critical if the agent handles sensitive information or could cause harm.
Simple Metrics: Easy-to-implement measures like median response length are good for evaluating and monitoring language models for improved usability and cost efficiency.
In all these cases, track performance as you develop your algorithms as well as in production over time, where the model is exposed to a constant stream of real-world data. This way, you can improve and refine your agent. This evaluation often leads to an iterative refinement process. You adjust the prompt based on the AI's performance, clarifying instructions or adding more examples until the output aligns better with your expectations. Treat LLM applications like software development where testing and feedback are crucial for improvement.

Terms
Ground Truth Evaluations: A method of evaluation where you create a set of test inputs and their corresponding ideal outputs (the ground truth) to score an agent's responses against.
LLM-as-a-judge: An evaluation technique where another LLM is used as a "judge" to score an agent's output against the ground truth based on your provided criteria.
Traces: The raw inputs and outputs of a model for a given interaction, which are important to view to understand the model's behavior and catch potential errors.
Consistency & Persona Adherence: An evaluation method that assesses whether an agent is staying within its defined role, even when asked about non-medical or other out-of-scope topics.
Robustness Testing: The process of assessing an agent's resilience against adversarial attacks, which is critical for agents that handle sensitive information.

Let's explore how to apply this technique with concrete examples across three different domains: coding, data analysis, and creative writing. For each of these, we'll look at a complete prompt, examining the role, task, and format or tone specifications. First, let's consider a coding task. Imagine we need a Python function that calculates a Fibonacci sequence using a specific optimization technique, memorization. Simply asking the AI to write a Python function for Fibonacci might yield basic code, but we want something robust and well structured, like a senior developer would produce. This is where the role comes in. Here's how we can construct a prompt for our AI agent acting as a senior Python developer. When we are working with an LLM's API, we'll normally specify a system prompt where we can include these general instructions before asking the real question in the user prompt. If you are using an LLM app, however, for example, ChatGPT, you will usually put both parts together in the single text field provided, which is a convenient way to prototype prompts before putting them in code. In this prompt, we start by assigning the role of senior Python developer in the system prompt. This immediately sets an expectation for the quality and style of the output code. We also provide additional crucial details about code structure, documentation, and adherence to best practices. Let's look at the second half of the prompt now. Here the task is clearly defined. Implement a specific function with a particular optimization. Finally, the examples, not completely shown, provide concrete input/output pairs, helping the AI understand the exact expected output format. This kind of detailed instruction enables agents to create code snippets from instructions effectively. Next, let's look at data analysis. Suppose we have sales data for a new product and need a summary of key trends and recommendations for the marketing team. A general AI might provide a factual summary, but assigning the role of a marketing data analyst can tailor the output specifically for audience and purpose. By defining the marketing data analyst role, we prompt the AI to interpret the data and frame its analysis through a marketing lens. This means it's more likely to focus on aspects relevant to campaigns and target audiences, rather than just raw numbers. We clearly state the task and specify that data is provided. The prompt specifies that the output be structured like a business report, easy to read with bullet points and provides actionable insights, precisely what a marketing team needs. The examples reinforce the desired structure for both findings and recommendations. In fact, we could provide an example format for the entire document for the LLM to read and follow. Finally, let's turn to creative writing. Suppose we want the opening scene of a fantasy novel in which a protagonist discovers a hidden magical object. Creative tasks often require significant guidance to achieve the desired style and atmosphere. Assigning the role of a fantasy author can be incredibly effective here. Here, we provide the task, as well as several details that will help shape the outcome. Specifically, we want the text to be in third person perspective, to also focus on sensory details, and to foreshadow possible danger coming from the artifact. How well does it work? Well, judge for yourself. "Dust swirled in the dim light filtering through cracks in the dead lake bed above as Zeb navigated the ruins. His boot nudged something smooth amidst the rubble, an obsidian shard, radiating a silence colder than stone." Is this a story you would continue reading? If not, I'm sure you could find a prompt that would write an opening line that you do like. As you can see, applying role-based prompting, combined with clear task definitions, specific format and tone requirements, and well chosen examples significantly enhances our ability to guide AI agents effectively across different domains. This layered approach provides a necessary structure and context for the AI to understand complex intent and produce outputs that are not only accurate, but also tailored to our specific needs. By mastering these techniques, we can unlock the full potential of AI agents in countless applications.
